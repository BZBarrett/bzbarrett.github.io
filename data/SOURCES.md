# Journey Studio data

## National parks

`national-parks.json` contains the 64 National Parks listed in the [National Park Service's National Park System directory](https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/national-park-system.htm), checked November 23, 2026. This includes [Chiricahua National Park](https://www.nps.gov/chir/index.htm). Other NPS designations are outside this checklist.

Names, states, and official page URLs come from that directory. Coordinates are representative park locations from the National Park Service's [Inventory & Monitoring park points](https://services1.arcgis.com/fBc8EJBxQRMcHlei/ArcGIS/rest/services/NPS_MonitoringNetworks_forNWMapsRepublish/FeatureServer/1), queried in WGS84 longitude/latitude. Gateway Arch uses the official Gateway Arch and Visitor Center point in the NPS [Public Points of Interest](https://mapservices.nps.gov/arcgis/rest/services/NationalDatasets/NPS_Public_POIs/MapServer/0) layer. These are overview-map markers, not entrances or navigation destinations.

Sequoia (`sequ`) and Kings Canyon (`kica`) are separate parks with separate NPS point locations; both official links lead to their shared `seki` website. American Samoa, Alaska, Hawaii, and the US Virgin Islands are included.

## United States outlines

`us-states.geojson` is converted from [`us-atlas@3.0.1/states-10m.json`](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/us-atlas@3.0.1/states-10m.json), with coordinates rounded to five decimal places and one degenerate three-vertex ring omitted. It contains 56 state, district, and territory features, including Alaska, Hawaii, American Samoa, and the US Virgin Islands. Coordinates are unprojected WGS84 longitude/latitude.

[US Atlas](https://github.com/topojson/us-atlas) distributes simplified U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundaries. Census geographic data are public domain. The US Atlas package is ISC-licensed:

## Satellite and topographic basemaps

Checked November 23, 2026. NASA GIBS [WMTS capabilities](https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov/wmts/epsg3857/best/1.0.0/WMTSCapabilities.xml) provides `BlueMarble_ShadedRelief_Bathymetry` (GoogleMapsCompatible_Level8, JPEG, 256-pixel tiles), with optional `Reference_Labels_15m` (Level13, PNG). [Blue Marble Next Generation](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/) is approximately 500 m global overview imagery. Attribution follows [NASA GIBS data-use guidance](https://nasa-gibs.github.io/gibs-api-docs/#data-use-guidance-and-acknowledgements); the labels additionally credit OpenStreetMap contributors and its copyright URL in exports. Neither mode promises recent imagery or street-level aerial detail.

US topographic tiles use the USGS National Map `USGSTopo/MapServer/tile/{z}/{y}/{x}` service at `basemap.nationalmap.gov`. Native levels are capped at 16 (verified available); deeper map zooms overzoom these tiles. The map and exported images credit the U.S. Geological Survey, National Geospatial Program, following [National Map terms](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-are-terms-uselicensing-map-services-and-data-national-map). US coverage is the intended use. These raster sources require internet and permit cross-origin canvas export. Devices without WebGL retain the explicitly identified local 2D overview.

## KMZ handling

`js/vendor/jszip-3.10.1.js` bundles JSZip 3.10.1, used under its MIT license (included as `js/vendor/JSZIP-LICENSE.md`). It was copied from the installed local runtime package. KMZ packaging and source backups run entirely in the browser; no uploaded travel data is sent to a conversion service.
